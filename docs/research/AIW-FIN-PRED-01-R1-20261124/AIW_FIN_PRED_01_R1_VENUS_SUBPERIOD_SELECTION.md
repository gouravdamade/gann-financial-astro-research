# Why Venus qualifies, and where the selection stops

**VENUS_AMONG_MULTIPLE_ELIGIBLE_PERIODS.** The six financial channels admit **Venus, Sun, Moon, Saturn and Mercury**. A separate Vimśottarī rule by Raman narrows general adversity to **Mercury, Venus and Moon** when Ketu is the malefic parent. All three are already in the financial set. Neither operation distinguishes debt from another difficulty or gives Venus priority. Raman’s own entry for Ketu–Venus begins with wealth and happiness.

## Input discipline

The same geometry is applied to all four plates. Ketu as the parent is supplied by the research question. The procedure does not prospectively select Ketu from all major periods. ## What the chapter selects

The six channels are A wealth-house ruler, B aspector of that house, C occupant, D aspector of its ruler, E companion of its ruler, F ruler of the second from the Moon. Count houses inclusively. On R44 they give Mercury, Saturn, Sun, empty, Venus, Moon, respectively. Under seven-graha full directed aspects and ordinary sign ownership Ketu has no direct route. The chapter assigns the child-only case limited second-house influence. Both rulers participating gives emphasis, parent only gives a feeble result. Neither participating supplies no selection by this operator. The text names three positive categories. The fourth is the logical outside case. The general planet catalogue and the importance of other ownership, location and aspects qualify the nature of results. On Chart 38 Raman specifically regards Venus as more effective than the other four. He identifies no reusable comparison function or ordering there. Transferring that superiority to Chart 44 would also change Venus’s role from second lord to companion. Dispositor inheritance is explicit **in Chart 40**. In a separately labelled analogy for Chart 44, Ketu could stand for Saturn, a direct participant. All five eligible children would then have a participating parent. That change still leaves the same competitors. Rahu also occupies the Moon’s second with Saturn. Route F asks for the **lord** of that sign. A node-aspect or automatic-proxy expansion requires a separate profile. ## Qualified parent–child rules

Raman classifies Ketu as naturally malefic. In that parent’s period his rule 13 singles out the rulers of the third, fifth and seventh stars counted inclusively from the birth star for a time of trouble. Punarvasu (7) gives Āśleṣā (9, Mercury), Pūrvaphalgunī (11, Venus), Hasta (13, Moon). His rule 14 separately covers mutual 6/8 and 2/12 relations. Moon in Gemini is sixth from Ketu in Capricorn. Venus in Libra is tenth, with Ketu fourth from Venus. **Moon alone meets the second geometry in this chart.** Rules 12–14 supply no preference among overlaps and no debt result. The apparent extra flag for Moon is no warrant to rank it either. [HPA VI p. 19/PDF 35, XII pp. 52–53/PDF 68–69, XXIV pp. 140–143/PDF 156–159](https://archive.org/details/hindu-predictive-astrology-b.-v.-raman/page/n157/mode/1up)

Three checks address the nature of the result.

1. Raman calls Venus functionally malefic in this particular chart on account of ownership of 3 and 10. Venus also occupies its own Libra with Mercury. Elsewhere in the same chapter the second lord joining the third lord in the third is a prosperity testimony in the discussion of the **second lord’s period**. Its D9 exception requires Mercury in 6, 8 or 12 from Jupiter. Mercury is third from Jupiter. The chapter’s explicit large-debt configuration puts the second lord in the eighth with its lord. Mercury is in the third. The favorable clause concerns Mercury, and the debt clause fails here. Neither uniquely selects the companion’s Bhukti. [Raman I, pp. 93–94 and 102–103/PDF 102–103 and 111–112](https://elibraryofyoga.com/server/api/core/bitstreams/2271a6e2-f4ca-49bd-9706-89cec2d6f5f5/content#page=102)
2. Raman’s own nine Ketu-child entries distinguish the children by name. Venus receives favorable financial testimony and later personal troubles. Ketu, Moon, Rahu and Saturn have explicit losses of wealth, money or property. Within the direct six-channel set, **Moon and Saturn** have explicit wealth-loss testimony in this catalogue. The author directs the reader to consider natal strength and life circumstances. He supplies no case-specific precedence between the catalogue and functional maleficence. [HPA XXIV, pp. 140–143, 165–167/PDF 156–159, 181–183](https://archive.org/details/hindu-predictive-astrology-b.-v.-raman/page/n181/mode/1up)
3. Santhanam’s BPHS II LIX.7–15 contains a genuinely parent-specific conditional loss rule. Venus is in its own sign and a quadrant from Ketu (10). It is third from the ascendant, and Mercury in Libra is its only companion. Neither relative 6/8/12, debilitation, a debilitated companion nor natal 6/8 is present. The favorable relationship in vv. 10–11 applies. *Phaladīpikā* XXI.67 lists interpersonal trouble under Ketu–Venus. The explicit wealth losses at 66, 69 and 73 attach to other children. These witnesses supply independently testable statements, each in its own context. [Santhanam II, pp. 718–719/PDF extract 135–136](https://sanjayrath.com/wp-content/uploads/BPHS-BJC2.pdf#page=135) · [Sastri 1937, pp. 238–240/PDF 275–277](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.92117)

## Disclosure after evaluation

The late Chart 44 narrative names Venus and reports loss of fortune and ₹50,000 of debt. Its explanation returns to Ketu in the sixth, its lord Saturn in the twelfth, and Saturn in the second from the Moon. It never compares Venus against Mercury, Moon, Saturn or Sun for this loss. In the earlier parallel Raman uses the same chart to illustrate the **temporary nature of authority** when the tenth lord is in the third. That is a reason for a rise and fall, with no specified Bhukti. The supported reconstruction is a set of eligible intervals plus a separately attributed case judgment. The historical amount remains an author report.
