# Which witnesses actually contain this nativity?

The earliest page image located for the matching nativity is in a **different book**. In the **second edition (1947)** of *Bhāvārtha Ratnākara*, confirmed on its title page (scan 1), Raman’s Chart **45** has the same ascendant, seven planets and two nodes, and the same unusual account of a once prosperous native. The upper diagram on that page, Chart 44, belongs to another person. The lower diagram and the adjacent prose identify this case independently of its number. [Raman translation and notes, ch. IX, stanza 6, printed pp. 78–79, scan 86–87](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.134838/page/n86/mode/1up)

**Acquisition record.** Searched date/time/amount combinations, both book titles, early parts and editions, separate horoscope accounts, chart errata, pañcāṅga and ephemeris references. The Archive volume titled for Santhanam is a different work. The Raman Archive and GitHub copies with an extra cover reproduce the same Word-derived layout (Chart 44 on PDF 112). The seven-page facing-page sample ends before the chart. The local prior HPA download is HTML, so the verified 310-page UBS scan above supplies the manual. Two DLI identifiers give the same early setting, and the BVRaman-labelled Archive mirror is byte-identical to 2015.134838. None increases the independent witness count. Google Books page-image requests for the two late books returned HTTP 403, with API lookup rate-limited. No manual corrections, dated forecast, owner’s birth entry or working sheet were located. No library was contacted.

Raman names Raphael’s and other standard ephemerides in HPA IX, p. 37/PDF 53. His own cited ninety-year tables begin in 1891. The Amsterdam library lists an 1888 Raphael volume, a candidate for collation only. No September page of a specifically Raman-used edition was recovered. [Amsterdam holdings](https://www.theosofie.nl/bibliotheek/collectie/randgebieden/ran-7-6-astrologie-tabellen/)
