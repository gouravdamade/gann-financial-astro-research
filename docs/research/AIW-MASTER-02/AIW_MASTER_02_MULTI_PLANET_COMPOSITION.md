# When concurrent testimonies admit a decision

**A newly located same-house decision rule.** The English BPHS Sudarśana chapter 74.1–14 constructs three concentric house series from Lagna, Moon and Sun. Vv7–10 supply context including the Sun in the first house and exalted malefics. Vv11–14 judge occupation, then aspects when unoccupied, then the lord when neither is present. With a mixed eligible group, majority of benefics or malefics decides. Equal counts invoke relative strength. Equal strength gives a mixed result. This is direct textual support for a majority and tiebreaker in a specific method. The translation does not specify how to aggregate heterogeneous strengths in a two-against-two tie. Verify the Sanskrit and handling of repeated identities across the three circles before admitting real fixtures. [Chapter 74, printed pp143–144](https://vedic-astro.s3.amazonaws.com/books/bhrihat_parasara_hora_shastra.pdf#page=143)

**Five simultaneous conditions.** Synthetic input within one circle and house: an empty house receives three eligible aspects, two benefic and one malefic after screening. Its lord is strong and a fifth planet supplies an adverse aspect to a different house. The first house resolves favorable (2 > 1). The lord fallback is inactive. The other house keeps its adverse testimony. If one benefic withdraws, 1:1 invokes strength. Stronger malefic gives adverse, equal strength mixed, missing strength unknown. A second route referring to the first benefic is not a third benefic. No whole-chart sign follows.

**G1 alternate profiles.** Hold the same benefic, malefic and mutual aspect fixed. Give both known strong status. Iyer’s v52 equates the described mixed configurations and includes co-occupancy; it leaves the actual value unspecified. Sastri/Bhat CIV.52 renders neutralization of the strongly opposed effects, permitting scoped cancellation. The Sanskrit digital witness 103.52 explicitly uses strength, aspect and parity language. A parity of results supplies no numerical zero. With strength missing the specific 1946 conclusion is undetermined. With only co-occupancy Iyer’s case survives, but one cannot insert an unobserved aspect into the alternative. Bhaṭṭotpala’s gloss and the definition of “strong” would decide whether a common operational predicate exists. The BPHS majority rule has a different object and cannot adjudicate this translation. **Higher-order rule.** Sārāvalī XVIII.20 assigns a mixed social description to natal Mercury, Moon, Mars, Venus and Saturn together. XVIII.21 exchanges Mars for Jupiter and describes ministerial standing. The four common members cannot distinguish those outcomes. Five binary pair weights inferred from the first description would also fire in unrelated charts. Chapter XX independently describes renunciation for some overlapping groups. Store both proofs when conditions are met. No automatic priority, four-of-five interpolation, sixth-member convention or transit transfer is established. **Implementation choice.** Addition is exact for eight equal Aṣṭakavarga testimonies about one planet/sign. Conditional rules express obstruction, aspect fallback and class-dependent modifiers. Hyperedges express indivisible configurations, with partial order across unrelated results. Keep the smallest sufficient sets of observed facts for each conclusion; two derivations of one assertion carry two proofs, one assertion. Key a conclusion by source profile, subject, target, domain, interval and effect. Different domains or opposite assertions survive. A decision operator must name its jurisdiction before it can consume them.
