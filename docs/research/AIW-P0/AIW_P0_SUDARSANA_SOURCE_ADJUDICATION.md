# Sudarśana house judgment

**Decision.** Admit the occupied-house, empty-house/aspect, and empty-unaspected/lord branches as textual rules. Admit the explicit 2:1 rule in the named English profile when the relevant testimonies are already classified. Equal-count comparison is described. A reproducible function for strength and a universal rule for repeated identities across rings remain open.

**Source identity.** The previously anonymous English wording can be traced to Gouri Shankar Kapoor’s volume II of the 97-chapter *Brihat Parasara Hora Sastra*. The volume II preface is signed 21 December 1987. R. Santhanam translated the first volume. Chapter 74, printed pp914–915, gives the judgment and pp916–919 its worked illustrations. A Sanskrit text of 74.5–16 is also available. A distinct *Subodhinī* commentary print places the topic in pūrvakhaṇḍa 51. These are identifiable textual profiles. Their relationship is insufficiently established to give an English expansion the status of a universal Sanskrit rule. | Question | Acceptable reading | Operational boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Three circles | Vv5–8 align the same numbered house from Lagna, Moon and Sun. The signs in that sector can differ. | Retain ring, sign, graha and aspect identity through evaluation. |
| Classification | Vv8–10 qualify Sun by house and an exalted malefic. Vv15–16 and the worked example use divisional placement. | “Malefic by nature” is insufficient to assign every vote. Medium is an observed category in the example. |
| Occupants and aspectors | V11 anchors an occupied house to its conjunctions. An empty house takes the effects of its aspects. V10 also recognizes aspects to occupied houses. | Use an occupant-first branch. Universal exclusion of all aspects once an occupant exists goes beyond these verses. |
| Eligible aspectors | Count bodies actually aspecting the specified house under the chosen dṛṣṭi convention and after classification. The worked empty third house uses Mars and Saturn. | The paragraph does not itself give a complete geometry or minimum aspect fraction. |
| Majority | V12 uses relative preponderance. Kapoor operationalizes this by numbers of qualified grahas. | Accept for homogeneous counts in this profile. A count of positive proof paths has no source warrant. |
| Ties | V13 asks for graded comparison. Kapoor expressly uses stronger side at equal headcount, mixed at equal strength. | The Sanskrit phrasing alone gives no measured strength unit, equality tolerance or rule for combining several strengths. |
| Fallback | V14 defers to the house lord when both occupation and aspects are absent. | An unexamined aspect is unknown. Establish absence before invoking the fallback. |
| Repeated graha | Kapoor’s worked example judges the same numbered house across rings and distinguishes qualified planetary roles. | No explicit tie case with coincident Lagna and Moon proves whether to duplicate or collapse a physical planet. Keep both references and withhold a multiplicity-sensitive verdict. |

**Three discriminating fixtures** use one empty house, eligible distinct aspectors, and supplied classifications. (1) Venus and Jupiter favorable, Saturn adverse gives 2:1, favorable in Kapoor’s reading. (2) Venus favorable and Saturn adverse, both aspecting, with Saturn independently judged stronger, gives 1:1 → adverse under the English tiebreak. Unknown strength makes this branch indeterminate. (3) With equality of strength explicitly stipulated, the same 1:1 yields mixed. Neither fixture 2 nor 3 establishes a strength evaluator. A compound two-against-two case with no agreed group measure must abstain.

**A worked alternative exists.** The *Subodhinī* page gives an example with a first-house sector across three rings and a two-benefic/two-malefic assessment. Its following Lagna calculation counts aspect quarters: Mercury 2 + Jupiter 4 + Venus 1 = 7 favorable, versus adverse contributions 1 + 3 = 4. It judges the favorable excess of 3, then proceeds to the lunar ring. This establishes an explicit unequal-weight procedure in that commentary example. It does not show that the later chapter's equal-count instruction uses precisely that operator. A 2:1 headcount and a 2:1 total in quarter aspects measure different things. [*Subodhinī* scan, page 437](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/पृष्ठम्:श्रीमद्बृहत्पाराशरहोराशास्त्रम्_(सुबोधिनीटीकासहितम्).pdf/४३७)

**Evidence required for full acceptance.** Collate the Sanskrit type in Kapoor at vv12–13 with a second print and reproduce one drawn chart with coincident reference signs. Seek an explicit worked comparison of two groups tied by number, showing each aspect amount and any separate bala. These dependencies concern house judgment only.
