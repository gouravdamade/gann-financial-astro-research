## Direct Sanskrit evidence and grammatical constraint

The stable verse is:

`पापदृक्पादहीनं तच्छुभदृक्पादयुक् तथा ।`
`बलैक्यं ज्ञेज्यदृक्युक्तमेवं खेटबलं भवेत् ॥ १९ ॥`

IAST:

`pāpadṛkpādahīnaṃ tacchubhadṛkpādayuk tathā |`
`balaikyaṃ jñejyadṛkyuktamevaṃ kheṭabalaṃ bhavet ||`

The compound `jña-ijya-dṛk-yuktam` identifies Mercury (`jña`) and Jupiter (`ijya`) dṛṣṭi as joined/associated with the `balaikya`. The Sanskrit securely licenses their inclusion/association. By itself, however, the compact compound does **not numerically state** `+60`, nor does it explicitly say whether Mercury/Jupiter are (a) excluded from the ordinary śubha/pāpa aggregate and then inserted whole, or (b) included in that aggregate and then additionally added whole. That distinction must come from commentary/recension or worked arithmetic, not from engineering preference.

### 2. The common Sanskrit does not itself adjudicate double-counting

Independent digital Sanskrit witnesses continue to agree on `jñejyadṛkyuktam`. This stabilizes the existence of the clause in the common text family, but not its numerical implementation. A translation that says “incorporating” and one that says “super add” are interpretive expansions of the terse compound; neither wording should be projected backward as if it were an explicit Sanskrit `punar yojayet` / fixed-unit instruction.

### 3. No evidence for fixed +60 emerged

Nothing checked this run changes the prior conclusion: there is no textual basis for a blanket +60 merely because the aspecting graha is Mercury or Jupiter. If a full-extra profile is ultimately selected, the defensible quantity is the **computed dṛṣṭi magnitude**, not an invented constant.

### 4. Ordinary aggregation remains substantially closed

No newly checked source contradicts the prior Jha/Raman/Jain evidence that simultaneous benefic and malefic contributions coexist arithmetically. No precedence/dominance rule surfaced. The unresolved issue is now specifically the `jña/ijya` treatment, not ordinary stacking.
