# Conflict is keyed to the planet, the matter and the period

**Bṛhat Jātaka VIII.23** distinguishes opposed effects of one graha from those of two grahas. In the former case the Sanskrit uses *sadṛśe* (equal or comparable) and *yad adhikam* (whichever exceeds). It gives no numerical strength metric. Aiyar renders superiority as two or more concordant yogas against one contrary yoga. Note (a) illustrates the same matter with gaining and losing wealth. Sastri translates the unequal case as the more powerful effect. These are different instructions when numerical superiority and qualitative strength disagree. [Aiyar 1905, printed p. 94, PDF p. 138](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c2/The_Brihat_jataka_%28IA_brihatjataka00varaiala%29.pdf#page=138) · [Sanskrit VIII.23](https://www.siva.sh/brihat-jataka/8/23) · [Sastri 1956, pp. 195–196](https://astrojyotish.info/literature/brihat-jataka-sastri/)

Aiyar's note immediately after VIII.23 distinguishes the chapter's own periods from the commonly used stellar periods described in his appendix-like note. Transfer of the chapter's conflict mechanism to every Vimśottarī parent/child pair requires independent authority. If an adverse child and favorable parent overlap, keep the two records with their lineages. The same applies to a favorable raw Aṣṭakavarga count, a transit with vedha and the adversity rule. Vedha may suppress the particular transit benefit to which it belongs. It supplies no ordering for the other records.

Two more clauses give actual period operators. In VIII.11 Aiyar reads the Moon at the beginning of a period. Cancer at that instant is a prosperity indication. Looking up the Moon every hour afterward would answer a different question. In VIII.20 the specified effects of houses, signs, aspects and yogas are assigned to the period of the most powerful participant, with Nābhasa yogas expressly excepted from the yoga provision. This permits a source-specific timing selection when the participants and winner are established. No passage here identifies Venus as the winner for Chart 44 or applies that selection to its Vimśottarī child. [Aiyar VIII.11 and VIII.20, pp. 88, 91](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c2/The_Brihat_jataka_%28IA_brihatjataka00varaiala%29.pdf#page=132)

For Chart 44, sound earning capacity and loss of retained wealth can both describe the native. Compare question IDs before declaring any conflict. The actual loss is attributed to Ketu–Venus by Raman. Applying a count of five positive and negative statements to this chart would discard his distinctions and would not reproduce his judgment.
