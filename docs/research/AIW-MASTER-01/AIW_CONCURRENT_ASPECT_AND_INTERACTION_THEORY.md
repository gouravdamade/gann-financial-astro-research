# Concurrent influence · what can actually be resolved

The first question at an instant is which propositions refer to the same subject, target and result. The second is whether the selected source tells us how those propositions interact. Coincidence in time answers neither question.

| Configuration | What the evidence permits | Mathematical treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Two planets affect different houses or outcomes | Each result can survive intact even if their descriptions have opposite signs | Product of target-indexed states. A chart-wide preference would require an explicit utility function |
| Two incoming dṛṣṭis on one target | Sārāvalī 4.32–33 distinguishes geometries and strengths. It does not in those verses prescribe a general net result | Preserve both directed edges and their aspect fractions. No sum, majority or strongest-wins |
| One planet occupies, rules, aspects and receives | Position can supply several antecedents. Lordship needs the selected ascendant/house scheme. Aspect received reverses the direction of a cast edge | Build separate role edges from one planet. Only a source-defined conclusion can count as an outcome |
| Favorable placement plus an adverse qualification | Iyer v53 defeats a previously assigned good result under specified conditions. Mantreśvara XXVI.6 gives an exact alternative rule within his own profile | Directed suppression of the specified result. Preserve its base, reasons and edition |
| A strong functionally adverse planet | Positional strength and subject-specific role can point in different interpretive directions | Retain the native strength unit and the chart-specific role. A sign × strength operation needs both an authority and a domain |
| A natal group with several members | A named conjunction can produce consequences absent from pairwise aspect descriptions | One hyperedge with all participants and its own domain-specific outcomes |
| A long occupancy and short obstruction | The longer condition can persist while its qualified application changes | Intersect the time supports only where a rule relates them. Overlap alone leaves the states independent |

## A worked source rule for obstruction

Phaladīpikā, 1937, XXVI.6, printed p288, expressly pairs favorable Mercury places **2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 11** with obstructing places **5, 3, 9, 1, 8, 12**, respectively. Count both from the natal Moon. Under this text the Moon is exempt as an obstructing planet. The Sun has a different exception involving Saturn in v3, and the Moon has an exception involving Mercury in v4. These are source-specific conditional relations between planets and places. This transit Vedha table does not require an SBC grid.

For h=4, inspect place 3. One eligible occupant is sufficient. Two eligible occupants do not double the obstruction. If only the Moon occupies place 3 and every eligible body has been checked, the guard clears. If only the Moon is known but the other bodies are missing, report unknown. Changing the Moon to Venus changes the result. In the real USD fixture Venus supplies this positive witness. A proof of existence can be complete despite missing other bodies. A proof of absence requires closing the eligible universe. The operative rule is in v6's text/translation, distinct from separately typeset comparative material.

## Reinforcement, conflict, and exact limits

- Iyer v20 makes Mercury in the second personally advantageous in one domain and adverse in another. A single rule can therefore be mixed before any other planet is considered. Repeating its v4 general classification alongside the detailed verse cannot create two favorable votes.
- Iyer v52 describes an equivalence among mixed aspect or occupancy cases. It gives no magnitude and no right to subtract one planet from another. V56 gives a single co-occupant a role in the Moon's character. Relative power when several co-occupants disagree remains unestablished here.
- In the Vyas Vedha setting v166 supplies isolated numerical modifiers. Two simultaneously true predicates can produce competing candidate results without specifying product, sum or priority. Evaluate both conditional branches, mark the joint operator unresolved.
- The Phaladīpikā **editorial** SBC exposition, printed p318, contains an actual count rule. It maps one through five malefic Vedhas in a battle to a series of qualitatively different outcomes. Its unit is the qualifying Vedha in that setting. Its ordered labels are neither equally spaced magnitudes nor an ordinary gochara aggregation. This is evidence of a local cumulative rule and a counterexample to a universal count.
- Sārāvalī ch18 v20 describes five specified planets together at birth and assigns distinct personal consequences. The transit of those five planets across an institution at a later date is a different antecedent. Certify conjunction semantics before making this hyperedge executable.

## When competing model operations are meaningful

Addition requires disjoint countable contributions or homogeneous additive quantities. Conditional weighting requires a common measurement scale and a demonstrable weighting rule. Multiplication works locally for a native factor already expressed against a native base. Min/max is justified only if a text selects an extremal result or the researcher labels it a sensitivity statistic. Partial ordering preserves comparisons within domains while allowing incomparability between domains. A state machine is useful for entry, exit and change of qualification. Source-defined periods may create hierarchy, but a slow interval has no automatic priority over a fast one. Reinforcement by several independent citations improves textual confidence, with no additional astrological contribution.

Every composite should answer “which conclusion changes if this fact is removed?” For v6 the removal of the only non-Moon obstructing occupant restores eligibility if the universe is closed. For an unsupported pair interaction the removal changes only the recorded geometry. That difference is testable without consulting prices.
