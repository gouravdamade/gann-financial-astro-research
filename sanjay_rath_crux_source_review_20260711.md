## Authority verdict

This is a substantial modern Jyotish synthesis, not a classical root text. It discusses and combines Parashari, Jaimini and other predictive methods, cites classical authorities, and supplies many worked charts. For this workspace it belongs in the `secondary_interpretive_commentary` layer.

It is useful for:

1. identifying timing doctrines and their dependencies;
2. learning how several chart factors are synthesized rather than read in isolation;
3. retrieving explanations for Dasha selection, Arudha, Argala, Hora Lagna, Sahams and house-specific timing;
4. locating candidate rules that can then be checked against root texts and deterministic tests.

It is not sufficient by itself to certify a formula, assign a USDJPY or Bitcoin direction, or validate a trading rule.

## High-value doctrine candidates for retrieval

### Dasha selection and timing

The book treats Dasha choice as conditional rather than assuming one system is universally sufficient. Its discussions of Vimshottari, Narayana and other Dasha systems are valuable as a decision-tree map. The RAG agent may explain which prerequisites a method expects, but deterministic code must calculate periods and the selected method must be versioned.

### Jaimini-style structural concepts

Argala, Arudha, Karaka and Narayana Dasha recur throughout the book. These are useful additions to the knowledge layer because they describe relationships that are not represented by a single planet-strength number. They must remain separate features with explicit conventions; they should not be collapsed into Shadbala or Western aspect orbs.

### Timing frameworks beyond a single transit

The opening chapters discuss Sudarshana, Sarvatobhadra and Sahams. These can help the agent explain why a transit or event is normally judged with natal structure and period context. They are candidate research modules, not immediate trade inputs.

### Financially adjacent houses

The second, seventh, tenth, eleventh and twelfth-house chapters are the most relevant to wealth, business, work, gains and expenditure. Their natal-event rules may supply explanatory vocabulary for reference charts. They do not directly establish whether a market aspect is bullish or bearish.

### Synthesis rather than isolated labels

The recurring methodological lesson is to combine chart promise, relevant significators, Dasha activation and transit timing. For the local agent this supports a structured explanation such as:

1. deterministic feature observed;
2. doctrine source and prerequisite;
3. same-family historical evidence;
4. market geometry and execution result;
5. uncertainty or conflict.

## Material that must not be promoted directly

- Retrospective worked examples are illustrations, not out-of-sample validation.
- A rule attributed by the author to a classical source must be checked against a traceable root text or edition before it becomes a doctrine lock.
- Mortality, medical, fertility and gender-prediction material is outside this financial research scope and must be excluded from generated advice and training examples.
- Natal wealth or career statements must not be translated into `buy`, `sell`, entry or exit rules without an independently specified market hypothesis and walk-forward evidence.
- OCR text is searchable evidence, not a faithful critical edition. Any exact wording or number must be checked against the rendered PDF page.
